The standing custom

What you send in your place

Vaurn abolished the personal duel in the ninth year of the Assizes, for the reason every city eventually finds: the people most often called to the field were the least able to stand on it. The law that replaced it did not abolish the duel. It abolished only the requirement that you be present at your own.

What you send in your place is called a second. The Registry will recognise four kinds and no others, and the four are not alternatives so much as four different theories of what a champion is. The Halls have argued this for two hundred years and each considers the other three a category error.

A second is not a weapon. A weapon is a thing you use. A second is a thing you become, briefly, in another room.

The Registry itself takes no position. It enters the second, assigns it a serial, and files the plate. Serials are permanent and are never reissued: quote one to any clerk in Vaurn, in this century or the next, and the same second will be drawn out for you exactly as it was first entered — the same horn, the same crest, the same wear on the same escapement. Nothing about a second is remembered. Everything about it is recoverable, which the clerks will tell you is a different and better thing.

The percentages beside each trait are the Registry's own, taken from the full roll rather than from the plates that happen to be famous. They are published because of the Lantern Street affair, in which a dealer sold the same unremarkable warden eleven times, each time as the last of its kind.
