# The Book of Seconds

### The Registry of Proxies, and the four things Vaurn will accept in your place

---

## I. The reform

Vaurn abolished the personal duel in the ninth year of the Assizes, for the
reason every city eventually finds: the people most often called to the field
were the least able to stand on it. Debt-holders called out debtors. Guilds
called out apprentices. A man with a good arm could hold an entire street.

The law that replaced it did not abolish the duel — the Assizes were not
reformers, they were administrators, and they had noticed that a city which
forbids a thing entirely simply loses the paperwork. What the law abolished was
the requirement that you be present at your own.

What you send instead is called a **second**.

## II. The four kinds

The Registry will recognise four kinds of second and no others. The list has not
been added to in two hundred years, though every generation produces someone who
tries.

The four are not really alternatives. They are four different theories of what a
champion *is*, and the Halls that keep them consider each other's positions not
merely wrong but confused — a category error, politely tolerated at the Assizes'
annual dinner and nowhere else.

**The Hall of Grudges** breeds. A grudge is a beetle, and it is carried to the
pit in a cloth by a breeder who is careful never to say aloud what the grudge is
about. The Hall's position is that a champion must be *alive*, must be able to
lose, and must not understand the argument. Anything else, they say, is theatre.

**The Hall of Faces** wears. A face is a helm and a coat of arms, and whoever
puts it on is nobody at all for the duration — not a person standing for you but
a *role* that you and your opponent have both agreed exists. The Hall's position
is that the champion should be a vacancy. The obvious objection has been raised
at every Assize since the reform and the Hall has never once answered it.

**The Hall of Hours** winds. An hour is a clockwork thing that will do exactly
what it was built to do for exactly as long as its spring lasts, and then stop.
The Hall considers this the only honest form of courage, on the grounds that it
is the only one that cannot be faked, bribed, or talked out of it. Their
detractors point out that it also cannot be praised.

**The Hall of Distances** names. Nothing is sent at all. A figure of stars is
nominated, both parties agree in writing to abide by whatever it does, and the
matter is closed, because a constellation does nothing, forever. It is the
youngest Hall, the cheapest, and by a wide margin the most used — which the
other three consider the strongest possible argument against it.

## III. Serials

The Registry takes no position. It enters the second, assigns it a serial, and
files the plate.

Serials are permanent and never reissued. Quote one to any clerk in Vaurn, in
this century or the next, and the same second will be drawn out for you exactly
as it was first entered: the same horn, the same crest, the same wear on the
same escapement, the same star faint in the same corner.

Nothing about a second is *remembered*. Everything about it is **recoverable**,
which the clerks will tell you — at length, if you let them — is a different and
a better thing. Memory is a claim about the past. A serial is an instruction.

## IV. The Lantern Street affair

The trait percentages printed beside every entry are a scar.

For most of the Registry's history the odds were not published. Rarity was a
matter of expert opinion, and expert opinion was a matter of who you drank with.
Then a dealer on Lantern Street sold the same profoundly unremarkable warden
eleven times in one season — a round sallet, a single slit, a plain field, the
commonest thing the Hall of Faces produces — each time as the last of its kind,
each time to someone who had no way to check.

He was not charged with anything. There was no law against being the only person
in the room who could count. The Assizes instead ordered the Registry to publish
the shares, taken from the full roll rather than from the plates that happen to
be famous, and to state plainly what a tier means: not that a second is unlikely
in the absolute — every second is astronomically unlikely, that is what six
traits do — but where it stands against its own hall.

The dealer's eleven wardens are all still on the roll. They are Common. You can
look them up.

> **A second is not a weapon. A weapon is a thing you use. A second is a thing
> you become, briefly, in another room.**
